The approach

The site is not a funnel. It is a conversion engine.

Lifeport sells to aircraft outfitters and specifiers, not walk-in consumers. Nobody gets talked into a purchase by a landing page. So the win is not consumer funnels, it is removing every point of friction between an interested buyer and your sales team, and never letting a captured lead go cold.

The rebuilt site should do three things exceptionally well: let buyers purchase commodity items instantly, let them request a quote on everything else with zero friction, and pull every lead into a follow-up system that keeps selling after they leave.
Product architecture

One catalog, two clear actions

Everything lives in a single product catalog, filtered by category rather than split into separate e-commerce and non-e-commerce sections. On any product, one clear action decides how the buyer proceeds.

Buy now

Commodity items

Off-the-shelf parts (replacement lamps, consumables) get a real Add to Cart and checkout. No purchase order, no salesperson, no paperwork. This is the e-commerce piece switched on for the first time.

Request a quote

Made-to-spec items

Seating, stretchers, attachment systems get a Request a Quote action instead of a price. The item enters a Quote Cart rather than a checkout, so the buyer can build a full request in one place.

  • Filters, not silos. A single filter toggles "available to buy now" versus "all products", so the catalog stays unified and easy to browse.
  • The flag is data, not design. Each product carries a simple buy-vs-quote flag, so the same page template serves both and nothing needs to be built twice.
The core mechanic

The Quote Cart

This is the single most important custom piece. Instead of firing one request per product, a buyer adds multiple products to a Quote Cart and submits once. Your sales team receives a complete, structured list of everything the buyer is interested in, not a scattering of separate enquiries.

Add to quote

Buyer browses and adds any made-to-spec products to a single quote request, the same way a cart works.

Submit once

One submission with the full line-item list, their details and any notes, instead of product-by-product forms.

The request lands as a draft order plus a CRM opportunity, and the assigned rep is notified immediately.

Recommended build. Drive the Quote Cart off Shopify draft orders. The quote becomes a real order your team can turn into an invoice in one click, and it mirrors into the CRM for follow-up. Best of both: clean for sales, and every quote is also a nurtured lead.

The follow-up engine

No captured lead goes cold

Every form on the site, quote cart, contact, consultation, technical enquiry, feeds one CRM. From there, the follow-up runs automatically and stays relevant to what each buyer actually wanted.

  • Segmented at capture. The form tells us the interest (VIP seating, aeromedical, repair, and so on), so each lead enters the matching email flow. Messaging is always specific, never generic.
  • Abandoned-quote recovery. Someone builds a quote or cart and leaves without submitting. A triggered sequence brings them back, the same mechanic that recovers checkout on major retail sites.
  • Reorder lifecycle. For consumables, an automatic reminder: "you replaced this around twelve months ago, here is the link to reorder." Repeat revenue with no manual effort.
  • Existing-client touchpoints. Past buyers stay warm with periodic, useful contact, not only transactional email.
  • On-site scheduling. A built-in scheduler books straight onto the sales team's calendar from the site. No third-party tool and no extra monthly cost.
On the page

Make it easy to take the next step

Calls to action, everywhere

Current product pages are text-heavy with few or no CTAs. Every product and service page should carry a clear action a third of the way down, mid-page and at the end: request a quote, talk to the team, book a call.

Live chat / assistant

An on-site chat or AI assistant to answer product questions and route buyers to a quote or a booked call. Scoped as a phase item, with rules set for a defence-adjacent manufacturer.

Visitor intelligence

Know when a target account is on the site

You asked whether you can be alerted when a major company visits, so sales can reach out proactively. Here is the honest picture.

  • Company-level: yes. Tools that resolve a visitor's company from their network footprint can alert you when a named account (for example a large airframer) is browsing, so your team can follow up. This carries a monthly cost and is scoped as an optional layer.
  • Individual emails: no. Reliably identifying the specific person and their email is not realistically possible, and pushing for it runs into privacy limits. The value is knowing the company and working the account from there.

Recommendation. Prove the core engine first, then add company-level visitor alerting as an optional layer once the pipeline is live.

Support

A focused document library, not a heavy portal

The support scope is deliberately tight, so it ships with the site instead of becoming a project of its own.

  • In scope: a document library, manuals, warranty and returns information, supplier information, recall notices. Straightforward to find, login optional.
  • Already native: order history, purchase orders and reordering from an account are built into the platform, no custom work needed.
  • Deferred: anything beyond the document library sits as a clearly-scoped phase two, not a launch blocker.
Traffic & structure

Point the traffic at pages built to convert

Two supporting pieces that make the engine perform.

  • Google Ads alignment. Ads currently point at existing pages. Once pages carry clear CTAs and match search intent, keyword-to-page relevance improves quality score and lowers cost per click. A focused audit will pinpoint the quick wins.
  • Navigation cleanup. Bring key services into top-level navigation rather than burying them, and present leadership, quality and certifications as sections on existing pages instead of deep third-level items. Cleaner paths convert better.
